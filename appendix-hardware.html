Appendix D

Robot Arm Hardware Reference

This appendix is a hardware reference for the Franka Emika Panda robot arm. It covers physical form factor, joint-level mechanical parameters, electrical supply and communication requirements, motion performance limits, and the safety standards the arm is designed to meet. Use it as a quick lookup when interpreting specifications in the main chapters or when planning a physical installation.

D.1 Form Factor and Physical Overview

A collaborative robot arm (cobot) is designed to work alongside people in shared workspaces. Its physical form factor — size, weight, mounting footprint, and reach envelope — determines what table or cell it can fit on, how much counterweight the frame must provide, and how close a human operator can safely stand during operation.

The Panda is a desktop-class cobot: light enough to be moved by one person (18 kg arm body) and compact enough to mount on a standard laboratory bench. Its aluminium housing requires only a 120 mm square base plate. Figure D.1 shows the key physical dimensions.

D.2 Mechanical Specifications

All seven joints are revolute (rotating around a fixed axis). Each joint has a physical range limit, a maximum angular speed, and a maximum output torque — the product of the gear ratio and motor torque. The first four joints (J1–J4) form the arm and carry the full weight of the wrist and payload, so they have substantially higher torque limits than the wrist joints (J5–J7).

Joint limits are hardware-enforced. The robot's firmware will trigger a protective stop if a commanded position, velocity, or torque exceeds the limits in this table. MoveIt 2 reads these limits from the URDF and will refuse to plan motions that exceed them, but direct libfranka code must check them manually.

Why does J4 have an asymmetric range (-176 to -4)? The DH frame assignment places the zero angle of J4 pointing "backward" relative to the natural arm posture. In practice the arm reaches a full elbow-up / elbow-down range; the numbers simply reflect the coordinate convention documented in Appendix B.

D.3 Electrical Specifications

The Panda arm connects to a separate Franka Control Unit (FCU) via a single proprietary cable. The FCU houses the power supply, the real-time computer that runs the 1 kHz control loop, and the Ethernet switch used by the Franka Control Interface (FCI). The arm itself contains no user-serviceable electronics.

Dedicated network interface required. The FCI link must use a dedicated Gigabit Ethernet NIC on the control PC. Sharing the port with other traffic (ROS 2 DDS, internet) introduces latency spikes that cause the robot to trigger protective stops. Use two separate NICs: one for FCI, one for ROS 2 DDS.

D.4.1 Per-Joint Torque Limits

The large torque gap between arm joints (J1–J4, 87 Nm) and wrist joints (J5–J7, 12 Nm) is the most important mechanical characteristic to keep in mind when programming. Impedance and force-control gains (Chapter 7) must be tuned separately for the two groups because their stiffness characteristics differ by nearly an order of magnitude. D.5 Safety and Compliance

The Panda is certified as a collaborative robot under ISO/TS 15066 — the technical specification that defines power-and-force limiting (PFL) operation. In PFL mode the robot monitors contact forces in real time and stops before force thresholds that could injure a human are reached. This is fundamentally different from a traditional industrial robot that stops only when it hits a hard limit switch.

D.5.1 Built-in Reflex Categories

The firmware monitors three independent conditions. Any single violation triggers an immediate protective stop: all joints decelerate under controlled torque (not a crash stop) and brakes engage within a few milliseconds.

Reflex TypeTriggered byDefault threshold
Joint torque reflex Measured joint torque exceeds expected (model) torque by the threshold Per-joint; default ~10–20 Nm above model prediction
Joint velocity reflex Joint angular speed exceeds the per-joint velocity limit Per-joint; see Table D.1 max speed column
Cartesian contact reflex Estimated external force or torque at the EEF exceeds threshold 10 N force / 10 Nm torque (configurable via libfranka)

D.5.2 Soft Stops vs. Hard Mechanical Limits

The Panda uses soft stops for all reflex-triggered halts: the controller decelerates the joints under active torque rather than cutting power. This preserves the gearboxes, prevents the arm from falling under gravity, and allows the arm to be moved back into the workspace without a manual reset in most cases. Hard mechanical limits exist only as a last-resort backup for extreme over-travel; reaching them requires a factory reset.

The 1 kHz control loop is what enables soft stops to be effective. At 1 kHz the controller can detect an anomaly and issue a corrective torque command within 1 ms — fast enough to limit contact force spikes before they reach unsafe levels. This is why the PREEMPT_RT real-time kernel is mandatory: a standard kernel with 10–100 ms scheduling jitter would defeat the purpose of the high-frequency loop.

Adjusting reflex thresholds in code: You can raise (or lower) contact thresholds using robot.setCollisionBehavior() in libfranka, or via the franka_ros2 parameter server. Raising them above the defaults is sometimes needed for high-speed pick-and-place but should be accompanied by a formal risk assessment per ISO/TS 15066.

Key Terms — Appendix D

Revolute joint
A joint that rotates about a single fixed axis; all Panda joints are revolute.
Payload
The maximum mass the robot can carry at the end-effector flange while meeting all spec limits.
Repeatability
The ability to return to the same pose repeatedly; distinct from absolute accuracy (how close to the commanded pose).
Franka Control Interface (FCI)
The 1 Gbps Ethernet protocol that carries real-time torque commands and sensor data at 1 kHz.
Protective stop
A controlled deceleration triggered by any safety reflex; different from an emergency stop (power cut).
ISO/TS 15066
The collaborative robot safety standard; defines biomechanical force limits for human-robot contact.
PREEMPT_RT
A Linux kernel patch that enables hard real-time scheduling; required for the 1 kHz FCI loop.
Power-and-force limiting (PFL)
The safety mode in which the robot limits contact force rather than relying on physical guarding.
